## 2. Does following the money work?

Stocks the funds bought returned +0.76% a month against stocks they sold over the next month (t = 1.88, 34 months), suggestive, but short of the usual t = 2 bar for a real effect. One month later the gap is +0.09% (t = 0.24), so anyone acting on the published portfolios finds little left to capture.

| Measured over | Months | Bought | Sold | All held | Bought minus sold | t | Months bought won |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Next month | 34 | 1.46% | 0.70% | 1.17% | +0.76% | 1.88 | 71% |
| One month later | 33 | 0.98% | 0.89% | 1.00% | +0.09% | 0.24 | 52% |

Based on 5,313 active trades from 2023-11-30 to 2026-08-31. 88% of sold stocks had a next-month price.

*Equal-weighted baskets from 11 funds at 2 AMCs, whose decisions aren't independent. Prices come from the disclosures, so a stock every tracked fund sold out of has no next price and drops from the sold basket. Portfolios are published about 10 days after month-end, so a follower can't earn all of the next-month return. Costs and taxes are ignored.*

## 4. Does the optimiser beat equal weight?

Fitted only on past data and refitted every 6 months across 17 funds, the max-Sharpe portfolio returned 15.5% a year against 16.0% for equal weight (gap t = -0.22). No evidence it beats simply splitting money equally.

| Portfolio | Return | Volatility | Sharpe | Worst fall |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Max Sharpe | 15.5% | 14.9% | 0.61 | -26.7% |
| Min volatility | 11.6% | 13.1% | 0.39 | -32.7% |
| Equal weight | 16.0% | 15.0% | 0.64 | -32.6% |

Tested from 2016-12-02 to 2026-11-06, with 20 refits and 43% average turnover per refit.

*The funds were chosen today, so the whole test benefits from hindsight about which funds survived and grew. It compares methods on that same group; it doesn't say what any of them will return. Costs of rebalancing and taxes are ignored, which flatters the optimiser because it trades more.*
